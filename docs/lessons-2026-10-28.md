# Lessons from the first scored call

**Call:** 2026-10-27, 20:55 ET, for the 2026-10-28 session.
**Result:** direction 2/4, close band 1/4, range 2/4 full (4/4 touched), mean absolute
close error 0.64%.

## What happened

The FOMC held the target range at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote. Cook and Hammack dissented
in favour of a 25bp hike. The statement added:

> "The accumulation of more information will help the Committee determine the policy
> stance appropriate to ensure a timely return of inflation to the Committee's 2 percent
> goal."

Equities read that as patience. ES opened at 7,761.75, made its low at 7,750.25 in the
first fifteen minutes, then rallied through R1 (7,773) and R2 (7,812), peaking at 7,819.5
around 14:35 ET. It settled at 7,783.25, up 0.32%. NQ did the same, peaking at 30,178 and
settling at 29,979.5, up 0.56%.

Metals went the other way. Gold settled at 4,051.6 (-0.56%) and silver at 53.457
(-1.54%) as the 10-year yield rose to 5.045%. Both then rallied into the 16:00 equity
close, which is why the 16:00 marks look better than the settlements.

## What worked

- **Gold was a clean hit.** R1 at 4,084 capped the high at 4,097, S1 at 4,035 held within
  1.9 points, and the settlement landed inside the 4,050–4,065 band.
- **Silver's direction and downside target were right.** It settled 0.72 below the
  predicted band, but the 53.00 support held within 0.41.
- **The range envelope was right.** All four sessions traded inside the published
  expected range at some point; ES and NQ exceeded the upper bound by 19.5 and 128
  points respectively, which is why they are marked partial.

## What failed

- **ES and NQ direction.** The call leaned neutral-bearish and expected a fade. The
  market rallied instead. The error was 33 points on ES and 195 on NQ.
- **The scenario buckets.** Only one of four settlements landed inside a published
  bucket. ES settled at 7,783, in the gap between the hawkish (7,720–7,745) and dovish
  (7,790–7,825) ranges. NQ settled at 29,979, in the gap between 29,700 and 30,200.
  Three narrow ranges do not tile the outcome space.

## Three lessons

1. **Label the bias from the modal scenario, not the expected value.** The
   probability-weighted close was slightly below the prior close, so the call was
   labelled bearish. But the single most likely outcome (a patient hold, 42%) was a
   relief rally. When the mode and the mean disagree, say so explicitly.
2. **One axis is not enough.** "Hawkish vs dovish" conflates two things: the policy
   rate path and the long end. A hold can lift equities through the front end while a
   rising term premium pressures gold. v0.2 needs a second axis for real yields/growth.
3. **Make the output a distribution, not three named boxes.** The next version should
   publish five overlapping price buckets that cover the space, with probabilities and
   a Brier score. Gaps are a design defect, not a market surprise.

## Concrete changes for v0.2

- Replace the three named scenarios with a five-bucket distribution per instrument.
- Add a real-yield axis: 10Y TIPS proxy, 2s10s slope, DXY.
- Publish a modal scenario and an expected close separately, with a note when they
  disagree.
- Add options-implied move (VIX for ES, GVZ for gold, VXSLV for silver) as a check on
  the ATR-based range.
- Score every call automatically and keep a running Brier/MAE table.
